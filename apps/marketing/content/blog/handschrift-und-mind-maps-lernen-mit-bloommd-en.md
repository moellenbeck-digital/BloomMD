# Handwriting and mind maps: how to learn with BloomMD

A thermodynamics exam is not about single formulas but about connections: what follows from the first
law, where does entropy come in, which processes are there? A mind map shows exactly that. With the
BloomMD app on iPad you build it from your notes, write the formulas in with Apple Pencil and review
the material in three views.

## 1. Organise the material as a map

Create a new map “Thermodynamics” or open the lecture note from your Obsidian vault. Every heading is
a node: *Laws*, *State variables*, *Processes*, *Cycles*. Below them you add the individual topics,
such as *Isothermal* and *Adiabatic* under *Processes*.

The map is not a separate document. It is the Markdown note itself – in Obsidian you keep reading it
as text with headings.

![The “Thermodynamics” study map on iPad with laws, state variables and processes as nodes](/blog/bloommd-ipad-map-en.png)

## 2. Write formulas by hand

Nobody likes typing formulas. Select the node *First law*, open **Handwriting** in the node editor and
write with Apple Pencil:

`ΔU = Q + W`

An arrow to the *W*, “work” next to it – done. The pencil writes with pressure, fingers pan and zoom
the canvas. The drawing is saved as an SVG file next to your notes and embedded in the node; Obsidian
shows it too.

A handwritten word can be linked to a note, for example “work” to your note **Work**. How to build
concise concept maps with this is shown in
[Concept maps with Apple Pencil in Obsidian](/en/blog/concept-maps-with-apple-pencil-in-obsidian).

![The handwriting editor on iPad with the formula ΔU = Q + W from the node “First law”; arrows point to “heat” and “work”, and the word “work” is linked to a note](/blog/bloommd-ipad-handwriting-en.png)

## 3. Review with the outline

A different view helps when reviewing. The **outline** shows the same map as an indented list, level
by level. You see at once which topics are thin, and tapping a row opens the node in the editor.

![The outline view of the “Thermodynamics” map as an indented list](/blog/bloommd-ipad-outline-en.png)

## 4. Quiz yourself

Two approaches work well:

- **Collapse branches.** Collapse the child nodes of *Processes* on the map; the node then only shows
  how many subtopics are behind it. Name them, then expand again and compare.
- **Presentation.** The presentation view shows the map node by node: the heading large, the note text
  below, a progress bar at the bottom. Phrase headings as questions (“What does the second law
  say?”) and put the answer in a child node. You then see the question first and move on to the
  answer only once you have answered it yourself.

## Everything stays in your files

Map, text and handwriting are Markdown and SVG files in your folder – on the device or in your
Obsidian vault in iCloud Drive. You don't need an account for this. How the app works with the vault
is described in [BloomMD for iPad: your Obsidian vault as a mind map](/en/blog/bloommd-fuer-ipad-obsidian-vault-als-mind-map).

The app for iPad and iPhone is a beta; the App Store release is being prepared.
